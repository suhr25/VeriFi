# SRIT India IPO: complete financial deep dive

SRIT India Limited

**Important:** SRIT India is not a conventional high-margin software/SaaS company. It is much closer to a **government-focused technology systems integrator**, executing large projects involving software, hardware, implementation, support and operations. That distinction is extremely important when analysing its margins, working capital and valuation.

The primary source for the analysis is the company's **DRHP/RHP**, supplemented by NSE, SEBI and financial databases. [Securities and Exchange Board of India](https://www.sebi.gov.in/filings/public-issues/feb-2026/srit-india-limited-drhp_99558.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

# 21. Why is cash flow negative?

The primary issue is working capital. SRIT needs to spend money to execute contracts before it receives payment from customers. This is especially important because the company is heavily exposed to government projects. A large amount of money can therefore sit in receivables, other financial assets, other current assets, and contract-related balances. The company's operating cash flow was hit by substantial working-capital movements. [StockAnalysis.com](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/SRIT/financials/cash-flow-statement/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

# 24. Why working capital is so important

Think about a government project: SRIT might win a ₹100 crore contract, need employees/subcontractors/hardware/software, spend ₹30-50 crore executing it, complete milestones, raise invoices, wait for payment, and eventually collect the money. During that period, SRIT's accounting revenue and profit can increase while its bank balance does not. That is exactly why PAT rising does not necessarily mean cash rising.

# 40. The fundamental contradiction in SRIT

SRIT has strong revenue growth, strong PAT growth, a large order book, low debt/equity, government relationships, and repeat customers — but simultaneously negative operating cash flow, negative free cash flow, high working-capital requirement, government/customer concentration, a declining order book, and limited pricing power. That makes SRIT a much more complicated investment than simply looking at its ₹43 crore profit.

## The single biggest takeaway

Do not analyse SRIT India like a normal IT/software company. The more accurate mental model is: a government technology systems integrator and project execution company with a significant working-capital financing requirement.

The headline numbers look attractive: ₹450 Cr revenue → ₹43 Cr PAT → ₹1,200+ Cr order book. But underneath that: ₹43 Cr PAT → -₹12 Cr operating cash flow. That gap is the central investment question. If SRIT can convert its accounting profits into cash, the stock could justify a significantly higher valuation. If profits continue growing but cash remains trapped in working capital, the headline P/E can be misleading.
